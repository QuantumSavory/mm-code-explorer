How to read the lattice

Every colored point is a physical qubit in the displayed parity check. Its color identifies the polynomial that contributes it. Faint points show the other qubits.

At each lattice site, small offsets distinguish qubit blocks. A qubit is specified by both its site and its block. The offsets are only for readability.

We display one representative per family at the origin. Translating its support modulo the lattice periods generates that family. Lines indicate support membership, not nearest-neighbor hardware couplings.

With t polynomials, q = ⌊t/2⌋. Blocks are q-element subsets B. X families are (q−1)-subsets R: Fⱼ contributes to B = R ∪ {j} at +e. Z families are (q+1)-subsets T: Fⱼ contributes to B = T ∖ {j} at −e. All coordinates are reduced modulo the periods.

The negative exponents in Z checks implement the circulant transpose. Identical terms cancel over 𝔽₂, including terms that coincide after wrapping. Family labels use this fixed Koszul convention; block order and X/Z naming can differ between publications.

Limits: 2–6 polynomials, 1–32 sites per axis, up to 12 fourth-axis slices, 4,096 lattice sites and 24,576 qubits. Code distances in presets are reported by the cited sources, not computed here.
